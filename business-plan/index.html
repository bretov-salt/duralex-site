01

Executive summary

DuraLex is a modular, AI-native suite that runs a law practice from the first call to trial. Ten modules share one secure platform: intake, same-day sending and signing of any form or document, knowledge of the file, knowledge of the law, drafting and document assembly, verification of citations, statutes and facts, court formatting and filing, hearing and trial preparation, an inbox that turns court, agency and client email, texts and calls into deadlines and team assignments, and billing with trust accounting. Firms switch on only what they need, each module for a small monthly fee per user.

The suite is practice-agnostic. Each area of law is configuration on the same engine, and 13 are configured today. Security comes before features, firm files stay in the firm's own cloud storage, and nothing the AI produces becomes final until an attorney accepts it.

DuraLex was built inside a working multi-practice law firm with offices in Philadelphia and Brooklyn and roughly 2,000 client matters. Most of the suite has already been exercised on that firm's real work: its AI practice toolchain runs about 75 documented practice workflows and 70 automated quality checks, including an eleven-gate review every court filing passes. DuraLex packages that proven know-how into software any firm can use.

DuraLex is built from the ground up by attorneys, for attorneys. In the finished suite, every notice, message and document will be pulled in and assigned to a team member with follow-ups, from engagement through calendaring, tasks, court management, trial management and invoicing to the final disbursement. It is built to be as easy for clients and staff as it is for the attorney: clients will get updates in their own language and sign on their phone, with texting to follow, and staff will work from one queue in which every task has an owner.

Security comes first and is designed for the quantum age. NIST published the first post-quantum encryption standards on August 13, 2024, and a June 2026 federal memorandum sets December 31, 2030 as the target for agencies to reduce quantum risk. Post-quantum protection is on the roadmap as a feature that will be enabled: hybrid post-quantum key exchange on DuraLex connections and encryption that can be upgraded without rebuilding. None of the eleven legal technology competitors reviewed in September 2026 claims post-quantum protection.

02

The core issue

Every notice, filing, medical record and court order that reaches a law firm has to be read by a person, retyped into one or more systems, and turned into deadlines and tasks by hand. The software firms buy stores what staff type in. The lawyer is left as the link between the documents and the software, and a single missed date or wrong fact can end a client's case and expose the firm to a malpractice claim.

  • Two or more systems, entered twice. A firm with several practice areas often runs a practice management suite plus specialist tools, and retypes the same client into each.
  • Deadlines tracked by hand. Filing bars, response dates, hearing dates and appeal windows are counted by people and entered into calendars by people. Administrative errors, mainly calendaring and missed deadlines, account for nearly a quarter of legal malpractice claims (ABA Profile of Legal Malpractice Claims, 2020 to 2023).
  • AI without accountability. AI now sold on top of these systems drafts text and summaries, but a lawyer still has to check where each fact came from before relying on it.
  • Files scattered across vendors. Documents are uploaded into each vendor's storage, so a firm's own record is split across systems it does not control.
  • Court and client messages outside the system. Court notices, texts, WhatsApp and social media messages land in personal inboxes and phones. Someone has to forward each one to the right person and remember to follow up. In a 500-firm secret-shopper study, only 33% of firms answered a prospective client’s email (Clio, 2024).

The need is most acute in small and mid-size firms. About 69% of private-practice lawyers in the United States work alone or in firms of two to ten lawyers. These firms carry the same volume of documents and deadlines as larger firms without the staff to absorb it.

03

The engine

At the center of every module is one engine that reverses the flow. The documents drive the case, and the software does the reading, recording and calculating that staff do by hand today.

Practice-agnostic

One engine for every area of law. Each practice area is configuration (pipeline, required documents, deadline rules, forms, party roles) rather than new code. One client record spans every matter, so a criminal charge can flag its immigration consequences.

Security-first

Security ships before features. Firm files stay in the firm's own cloud storage. Two-step sign-in, access by matter assignment, a log of who viewed what, encrypted sensitive fields, and no pooling of one client's data into another's work. Built for the quantum age: post-quantum protection, a future feature to be enabled, will add hybrid post-quantum key exchange on DuraLex connections and upgradeable encryption, aligned with NIST FIPS 203.

AI-native

AI works inside every matter. Each fact carries its source, page, confidence and any conflict. Nothing enters the record until an attorney accepts it. Citations are verified before a draft reaches the lawyer, and AI cost is capped per matter. DuraLex picks the AI model for each task automatically, a lighter model for routine sorting and a stronger one for drafting, to control cost.

Connected and assigned

Email and the firm’s own storage feed one record today; court notices, texts, calls, social media messages and accounting are added as they are connected. Every item is matched to a matter, assigned to a team member and followed up until it is done, from engagement to disbursement, in every practice area.

The first product: any form or document, signed the same day

Firms lose clients and weeks between the consultation and the signed paperwork. Send and sign fills any form or document from what the firm already knows about the client (fee agreement, government form, medical release, declaration, authorization) and sends it the same day by text or email, for the client to read and sign on a phone, in the client's language side by side with English. The fee agreement is the flagship: scope and exclusions fitted to the matter, the fee terms the jurisdiction requires in writing, current government fees with the date checked, and a drafted amendment, sent the same way, whenever the law, a fee or the scope changes. Nothing goes out without the attorney's approval. It works in every practice area, which makes it the easiest first purchase and the door to the rest of the suite.

05

Why now

  • AI can now read legal documents reliably, and can point to the page it read. That makes it possible to build a system of record from documents instead of from data entry, as long as a lawyer confirms each fact.
  • Investors are funding the category. Legal technology drew about $6 billion of venture investment in 2025, and AI companies built for law firms raised seed and Series A rounds throughout 2024 to 2026 (Section 07).
  • Buyers are paying for automation. Relativity bought Gavel, a document automation company used by nearly 2,000 legal organizations, in June 2026. Getting documents like engagement letters out the door faster is worth acquiring.
  • Incumbents are adding AI to existing products. Clio launched Clio Work as a standalone AI workspace at $199 per user per month in April 2026 and added Clio Docket for court monitoring in August 2026. The large suites are layering AI onto data-entry systems; DuraLex starts from the documents.
  • The quantum clock has started. NIST finalized its first post-quantum standards on August 13, 2024, and a June 2026 federal memorandum sets a December 31, 2030 target for agencies. Client files must stay confidential for decades, so data taken today is at risk later. Google and Microsoft have not yet documented post-quantum protection for Gmail, Drive or Microsoft 365, and no legal practice competitor we reviewed claims it.
  • Small firms are ready to switch. Firms of every size now expect AI, and the firms least able to absorb manual work are the ones with the most to gain.

  • Practice management suites (Clio, MyCase) are the systems of record for most small firms. Clio is the strongest competitor: it serves every practice area, fills state and USCIS forms from matter data and sends them for e-signature by email and text, cites its AI findings to the source, and checks citations for good law in Clio Work.   • Single-practice tools (Docketwise for immigration) go deep in one area. A firm with several practice areas runs several systems.
  • General legal AI assistants (Harvey) draft and research across practice areas but are not the firm's system for matters, deadlines and billing.
  • Document automation is being bought. Relativity acquired Gavel in June 2026. Gavel automates documents such as engagement letters from firm templates and questionnaires, and was used by nearly 2,000 legal organizations; financial terms were not disclosed. DuraLex goes further: any form or document is filled from the matter itself, fee agreements are checked against the jurisdiction's fee rules, everything is translated, sent by text or email, signed on the phone, and amended when the law or fees change, inside the same suite that runs the rest of the matter.

11

Security and trust

Law firm buyers ask about security first. Client files are confidential under the Rules of Professional Conduct, and many contain information that can endanger clients if disclosed. DuraLex treats security as part of the product. Its design:

  • Firm files stay in the firm's own cloud storage (Google Workspace today, Microsoft 365 planned); DuraLex organizes and reads them.
  • Staff sign in through the firm's Workspace with required two-step verification; access by matter assignment; a log of who viewed which matter; automatic logout.
  • Sensitive identifiers encrypted field by field; privileged AI output kept in the client's own folder.
  • Developers never hold production access; all test environments use synthetic data.
  • Written terms with AI providers that client data is not retained or used for training; an external penetration test and SOC 2 readiness before outside firms go live.
  • Post-quantum protection (future feature). To be enabled: hybrid post-quantum key exchange (X25519MLKEM768) on DuraLex connections, keys held in a key management service that supports ML-KEM, and a design that lets encryption be upgraded, aligned with NIST FIPS 203. DuraLex will not claim that email, court systems or other providers in the data path are post-quantum protected.
  • Single sign-on. Sign-in through the firm’s Google, Microsoft or Apple organizational account, so access ends the moment the firm removes a user.
  • Secure client, attorney and court communication. As each connection is added, messages, documents and court notices will stay inside the matter, encrypted in transit, with a record of who sent and opened what.

Trust is also the moat: page-cited facts, a rules library per practice area, filing checks, and files that never leave the firm's control are harder to copy than a feature list.
